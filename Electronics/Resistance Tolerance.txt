Resistance Tolerance

Tolerance tells us how much the actual resistance value can vary from the marked/nominal value.

For example, a 100 Ω ±5% resistor means its actual resistance can be between:

Minimum = 100 − 5% = 95 Ω
Maximum = 100 + 5% = 105 Ω

So the actual value can be 95 Ω to 105 Ω.

Common resistor tolerance values
Tolerance	Meaning
±1%	Precision resistor
±2%	High accuracy
±5%	Very common
±10%	Less accurate
±20%	Low accuracy
Formula

Actual range = Nominal value ± (Nominal value × Tolerance / 100)

Example: 1 kΩ ±10%

Range = 1000 ± 100 Ω
➡️ 900 Ω to 1100 Ω

💡 Exam point: The gold band on a resistor usually represents ±5% tolerance, while silver represents ±10%.

colours value

Sure. For a 4-band resistor, the colour bands represent:

Colour	Digit	Multiplier	Tolerance
Black	0	×1	—
Brown	1	×10	±1%
Red	2	×100	±2%
Orange	3	×1,000	—
Yellow	4	×10,000	—
Green	5	×100,000	±0.5%
Blue	6	×1,000,000	±0.25%
Violet	7	×10,000,000	±0.1%
Grey	8	×100,000,000	±0.05%
White	9	×1,000,000,000	—
Gold	—	×0.1	±5%
Silver	—	×0.01	±10%

Example 🔴

Brown – Black – Red – Gold

Black = 0
Red = ×100
