Resistance, Capacitance and Inductance

These are the three basic passive electrical components used in electronic circuits.

Component	Symbol	Unit	Main function
Resistance (R)	R	Ohm (Ω)	Opposes current
Capacitance (C)	C	Farad (F)	Stores electrical charge
Inductance (L)	L	Henry (H)	Stores energy in a magnetic field
1. Resistance (R)

A resistor opposes the flow of electric current.

Formula:

$$ V = IR $$

Where:

V = Voltage
I = Current
R = Resistance

Example:
If \(V=10V\) and \(R=5Ω\):

$$ I=\frac{V}{R}=\frac{10}{5}=2A $$

Key point: Resistor converts electrical energy mainly into heat.

2. Capacitance (C)

A capacitor stores electrical energy in an electric field.

Formula:

$$ Q=CV $$

Where:

Q = Charge
C = Capacitance
V = Voltage

For a capacitor:

$$ i=C\frac{dv}{dt} $$

Important behavior:

Allows AC to pass depending on frequency.
Blocks steady-state DC after it becomes fully charged.
Used for filtering, coupling, decoupling and energy storage.

Example: A capacitor in a power supply can reduce voltage fluctuations.

3. Inductance (L)

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field when current flows through it.

Formula:

$$ V=L\frac{di}{dt} $$

Where:

V = Voltage
L = Inductance
\(di/dt\) = Rate of change of current

Important behavior:

Opposes sudden changes in current.
At steady-state DC, an ideal inductor behaves approximately like a short circuit.
Used in filters, transformers, oscillators and power converters.
