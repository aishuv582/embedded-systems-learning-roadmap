A transistor is a semiconductor device that can work as either a switch or an amplifier, depending on how it is biased.

1. Transistor as a Switch

When used as a switch, the transistor operates in two states:

Cut-off Region (OFF State):
Base current (\(I_B\)) = 0
Collector current (\(I_C\)) = 0
Transistor behaves like an open switch.
Saturation Region (ON State):
Base current is sufficient.
Collector-emitter voltage (\(V_{CE}\)) is very low (about 0.2 V for silicon BJTs).
Transistor behaves like a closed switch.
Applications
LED switching
Relay control
Motor control
Digital circuits
Microcontroller outputs

Example: When a small current flows into the base of an NPN transistor, it allows a much larger current to flow from collector to emitter, turning on an LED.

2. Transistor as an Amplifier

When used as an amplifier, the transistor operates in the active region.

Working Principle
A small AC signal is applied to the base.
A larger current flows through the collector.
The output signal is an amplified version of the input signal.
Types of Amplifiers
Voltage amplifier – increases voltage.
Current amplifier – increases current.
Power amplifier – increases power.
Applications
Audio amplifiers
Radio receivers
Television circuits
Communication systems
Sensor signal amplification
Difference Between Transistor as a Switch and as an Amplifier
Transistor as a Switch	Transistor as an Amplifier
Operates in cut-off and saturation regions	Operates in active region
Output is either ON or OFF	Output varies continuously
Used in digital circuits	Used in analog circuits
No signal amplification	Amplifies weak signals
Acts like an open/closed switch	Acts like a signal amplifier
